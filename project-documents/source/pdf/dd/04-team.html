4.1The agent team and its workflow

A founding team of five agents, each with its own colour and craft

Chief of Staff
the manager

Holds no area of its own. Turns the brief into the decision at stake, delegates, reviews, settles conflicts and writes the strategy.

  • the plan and each task
  • a score and feedback on every report
  • a decision on every conflict
  • the strategy and the proposal's summary
Reasoning: medium · its calls are the team's decisions
Marketing Strategy
a go-to-market lead

Has launched products on small budgets. Judges customer niches, positioning, demand and the go-to-market.

  • one beachhead niche, and who buys now
  • positioning against what buyers use
  • one or two affordable channels
  • the cheapest test of demand, with its threshold
Reasoning: minimal · reads competitor profiles
Financial Planning
a startup CFO

Builds lean launch budgets. Judges start-up costs, pricing, unit economics and an affordable start.

  • one pricing model and price point
  • a first-phase budget, line by line
  • gross margin per customer
  • customers to break even, and runway
Reasoning: minimal · reads competitor profiles
Technical Feasibility
a pragmatic CTO

Ships first versions with very small teams. Judges what to build first, the tools it needs and the technical risk.

  • the thinnest demo of the core value
  • build or buy, naming the services
  • the effort, in weeks
  • the riskiest assumption, and a cheap test
Reasoning: minimal
Risk & Compliance
a risk and compliance lead

Works with early-stage companies. Judges privacy, regulation, reliability, delivery and client-management risk.

  • the risks that could stop the launch
  • how likely and how severe each is
  • a safeguard each, and v1's limits
  • what the law requires, apart from good practice
Reasoning: minimal
One node, four roles. The specialists share one implementation and differ only in their role text (decision D4). Each is told to be specific to the venture, show the arithmetic behind any number, mark assumptions, cite research as [n], lead with its recommendation and keep to about 250 words; the model treats the length as a target, not a hard cap.
Web research is a tool, not an agent. It is drawn in slate grey: searches through Azure OpenAI's Bing or Tavily, deep research, and official figures from FRED. Each agent's colour follows it everywhere: progress, reviews, citations, the Monitor and the PDF.

4.2The agent team and its workflow

Plan, act, check: a LangGraph state machine with bounded loops

11 nodes, 40 edges. Three edges always run (start to plan, critique to resolve, synthesis to end); the other 37 are routes the state chooses.

Five routers. After the plan, the look-ups and research, the review, a specialist and the resolve, a function reads the state and names the next step or steps (graph.py).

Parallel by design. A router that returns several names starts those steps at once: the assigned specialists, or the look-ups beside deep research.

Always ends. One revision after review and one critique round, both settings; a revised report then goes straight on.

4.4The agent team and its workflow

Delegation: the Chief of Staff states the decision, then assigns only who it needs

The decision first. Before delegating, the Chief of Staff works out what the founder needs: the decision at stake, the constraints that bind it (budget, timeline, team, regulation, competition) and what is uncertain. The objective is that decision, in one sentence.

Only the specialists it needs. A whole idea goes to all four. A narrower request goes to the specialist whose area it asks about, plus the one who owns the plan it refers to: risks in a go-to-market plan go to Risk and Marketing. Each task is one or two sentences: the question for this venture and the constraint the answer must respect.

Typed, then routed. The reply is a DelegationPlan whose specialist field can only be one of the four names; repeats for one specialist are joined, and an empty plan assigns all four, so a request is never dropped. A router then starts only the assigned specialists; the rest make no call at all.

Follow-ups reassign. Shown the original request, the follow-up and who already reported, the Chief of Staff reassigns only the specialists who own what changes; the others keep their reports.

4.5The agent team and its workflow

Check, then cross-check: weak reports go back, and conflicts are settled

Review
Plan–Act–Check, by the Chief of Staff
5ready to use as it is
4useful and specific, minor gaps
3misses something the task asked for, or its figures contradict
2largely generic or off-task
1wrong or unusable

A score of 4 or more passes, and the code decides that from the score: a model once scored a report 4 and still rejected it. Below 4, the report goes back with one to three sentences of feedback and is rewritten whole, once. Only rejected reports cost another call; a round's reviews run at the same time.

Critique
the specialists check each other

Each assigned specialist reads the others' reports and flags a clash only where another recommends something that contradicts its own recommendation, inside its own area, so the founder could not follow both: a price, a budget, a timeline, the first offer's scope, a channel, a safeguard.

At most two each, the most consequential first; differences of emphasis or wording do not count. A lone report has nothing to be checked against, so the critique is skipped.

Resolve
the Chief of Staff decides

All of a round's conflicts in one call, a clash raised from both sides counted once. It weighs the founder's hard constraints first, then the evidence, then which option keeps the most choices open; law and safety outrank speed and revenue. Each decision names the one specialist who must revise.

4.6The agent team and its workflow

Synthesis: one strategy the founder can act on, built only from the team's work

The Chief of Staff merges the reports, and its own decisions on their conflicts, into one launch strategy, in this order:

  1. The recommendation: go, go with conditions, or not yet, with the main reason.
  2. The few facts from the reports that drive it.
  3. How each disagreement was settled, following its own decisions.
  4. The key risks, and how the plan contains them.
  5. Next steps, each with the area that owns it and a timeframe.
  6. Early metrics that show whether it is working.

The budget, prices, scope and timeline must agree wherever they appear. It adds nothing the specialists did not report, keeps the citation on any figure that has one, and names any report that could not be produced. It aims at 500 words.

Free text, by design. The strategy is Markdown for a person to read, so it is the one Chief of Staff output without a schema. Its structure comes from the prompt; the optional LLM judge scores its constraints, consistency, actionability and grounding.

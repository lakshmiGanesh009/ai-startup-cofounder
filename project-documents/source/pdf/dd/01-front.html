1.1Executive summary

One brief in, one launch strategy out, from a team that checks its own work

The AI Startup Co-Founder Team turns a founder's brief into a launch strategy the way a real founding team would. A Chief of Staff works out the decision at stake and delegates; Marketing, Finance, Technical and Risk & Compliance specialists analyse it at the same time; the Chief of Staff scores every report against a rubric and sends weak ones back; the specialists cross-check each other and the Chief of Staff settles their conflicts; then it writes one strategy that leads with go, go with conditions or not yet.

It is a LangGraph state machine behind a FastAPI service, with a Next.js app that shows the team at work, live, on the graph it runs. Before the team starts it can research the brief on the web and ask the founder what only the founder knows; while it works it reads official figures through an MCP server; afterwards the agents write an investment proposal whose financial figures are worked out in code, and the founder controls every change to the document.

It is built like a product: sessions and reports in PostgreSQL, an API key the browser never sees, rate limits, prompt guardrails, structured logs, Prometheus metrics, Langfuse tracing and evaluation, Docker and GitLab CI, and 578 automated tests.

On type safety, in brief

Every API request is a Pydantic model with enforced limits, and every decision the agents make (plans, reviews, conflicts and their resolutions, look-ups, the proposal's parts, answers and the judge's scores) comes back as a Pydantic model the model was constrained to produce: 17 of the app's 22 prompts. The four specialist reports and the final strategy are free text by design. Part 6 sets out the audit, call by call, including two gaps worth closing.

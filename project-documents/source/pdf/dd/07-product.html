7.1The product

The Evaluate page: write a brief, watch the team work, read the decision

  1. Compose. A brief of your own, a decision template (five) or a worked example (three); optional context; your files and earlier reports. "Research it and check the brief first" is on by default; deep research is a switch. ⌘/Ctrl+Enter sends it.
  2. Watch. The progress card fills in step by step, each with what it did ("Reviewed 4 reports: approved 2, sent back…"), who is working now and the time taken. Live graph opens the graph itself, lit up as it runs.
  3. Read. The strategy, set as a memo, leads with its verdict, the reviews beside it. Below: the specialists' reports, the research, the plan, the reviews round by round, the conflicts and the run's cost, step by step, with a link to its trace.
  4. Follow up. The session is kept in the browser tab: a follow-up reruns only the agents it concerns, and "Was this strategy useful?" records the founder's verdict.

The sidebar sets the reasoning of the specialists and of the Chief of Staff, where web research runs, and light, dark or the system's appearance.

7.2The product

From a strategy to an investment proposal, its money worked out in code

On demand, the agents write a session's report up as an investment proposal (executive summary, market and marketing, technical and operational architecture, financial plan, risk matrix, KPIs), each part structured data written by the agent that owns it.

Three waves, so the figures agree

Marketing Technical Risk at once, then Finance with the market and technical parts in front of it and their costs summed, then the Chief of Staff's summary and KPIs, with the investment metrics.

Numbers in code, not prose. Finance gives the drivers (customers, fixed costs, one-off fees, prices); code works out revenue, operating costs, NPV, IRR, payback and break-even. Checks compare year one's fixed costs with the launch costs, year three's revenue with the obtainable market, and average with year-end customers; a failing check sends Finance back once, and any still failing is shown in the PDF and on the page.

The PDF is drawn on the server with fpdf2: charts from the figures (market sizes, the funnel, the roadmap, costs, revenue against costs, the cash position, a risk heat map), every section labelled with its agent or "calculated", assumptions highlighted. Raw HTML in a report is printed as text and no image is fetched. The recorded run's proposal took 77 s; its PDF runs to 26 pages.

7.3The product

Document control: exact changes by code, every one a draft you accept

  • SettingsThe currency of every amount, the units, who it is prepared by, and the team's sections to leave out belong to the document. Amounts are plain numbers, formatted in its currency everywhere.
  • Currency, by codeA conversion multiplies every figure by the rate (today's, from the European Central Bank, filled in) and rewrites every amount in the text: symbols, codes and names, "$15k", "$10–20k", but not another currency or a year. 22 currencies.
  • Units, by codeAreas, distances, weights, volumes, speeds and temperatures, imperial or metric, only where the unit cannot be mistaken ("5m" might be five million).
  • Text, by codeFind and replace, redact names and figures, or remove a passage, across the title, the objective, the team's sections and the proposal's prose; never a fixed choice such as the verdict.
  • One passageThe agent who wrote a text rewrites only it, or one paragraph of it; code splices it back and leaves the rest as it was.
  • DraftsEvery change the team or a tool makes comes back as a draft: each changed field before and after, words struck through and added, amounts in both currencies, and the draft's own PDF. Nothing is saved until it is accepted, from the version it was made from. A section edited by hand saves at once.
  • VersionsEach save keeps the version it replaces, the last 30, with the change that made it; any can be looked at and restored.

7.4The product

The front end: an editorial design, live progress, and no key in the browser

Next.js 16 and React 19, three pages: Evaluate, Reports, and How it works with its Monitor. The pages call the API through the app's own route, whose server adds the API key, forwards only the routes the pages use, caps request bodies, and streams an evaluation's events as they arrive. Every page carries a content security policy and the usual security headers.

Live progress is a server-sent event stream, read with fetch since an EventSource can only send GET requests. The live graph is Mermaid's drawing of the real graph, with each node and edge lit up from the step stream alone.

The design system lives in tokens: warm paper and ink, one navy accent, hairline rules instead of boxes, Newsreader for the documents the agents write and Geist for the rest. Each agent's colour was validated beside its neighbours, in light and in dark; status colours always sit beside words.

Checked like the backend: 180 tests in 25 files with Vitest and Testing Library against a fake backend, 96% of lines covered, plus type checks and lint; and the UI steps were checked from live screenshots, light, dark and at phone width.

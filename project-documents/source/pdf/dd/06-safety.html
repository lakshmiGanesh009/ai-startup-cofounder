6.1Correctness and safety

Typed at every boundary: what comes in, and every decision that comes out

Requests in: constrained

Every body that starts model work is a Pydantic model with enforced limits: a prompt of 1 to 8,000 characters, context fields up to 4,000, at most 10 answers, 20 facts and 50 sources, each an http(s) address; session ids by pattern; reasoning efforts from a fixed list; a conversion rate above 0 and below 1,000,000. Anything else is answered 422, naming the field.

Checked on the wire

Captured offline for this audit, with the real client and a stand-in server: a DelegationPlan call is sent with response_format json_schema, strict: true and the four names as an enum. A reply naming a fifth specialist, "sales", is rejected by Pydantic: "Input should be 'marketing', 'finance', 'technical' or 'risk'".

Typed in storage

The checkpointer's serialiser lists the 13 state models a session may hold. Reports, their settings and the proposal are Pydantic models too, and old saves still load: money fields renamed since are read through aliases.

6.3Correctness and safety · the audit's findings

So: Pydantic everywhere a decision is made, with two gaps worth closing

The answer to "is every model call type-safe and constrained?" is: every decision is typed and every request constrained, but not every call. These are the exceptions, how far they matter, and the fix for each.

1 · Ranges and counts live in descriptions
gap · low risk today

"Score 1 to 5", "at most two conflicts" and "two to four searches" are written in the fields' descriptions, not as constraints. The model is guided by them, and code enforces most of them afterwards (the caps, the judge's clamp, approval from the score), but a review scored 9 would be accepted, and counted as approved.

Fix: add validators that clamp or reject out-of-range values (AfterValidator, or Field(ge=1, le=5) once the deployment's strict mode is shown to accept those keywords), with a test for each.

2 · A schema violation is not retried
gap · rare in practice

structured_invoke retries a reply that does not parse and one the content filter withheld. A reply that breaks the schema is rejected inside the SDK's own parsing, as a Pydantic ValidationError, which is neither retried nor among the errors a review or critique is skipped for, so it would end the request with a 500. Strict decoding makes such a reply practically unreachable on Azure.

Fix: catch ValidationError in structured_invoke, retry once, then raise LLMReplyError, so it is handled like any unusable reply.

3 · Five prose outputs, by design
a choice, with its own safeguards

The four reports and the strategy are for people to read, so they are Markdown: shaped by their prompts, cleaned of fence markers, scored by the review (the reports) and optionally the judge (the strategy), and a failed report becomes a named gap. A schema for them (a recommendation, points, assumptions) would buy structure at the cost of natural prose and more tokens.

4 · Research JSON is typed at the edge
a choice · low risk

A competitor profile is enforced by the provider's schema, parsed as JSON, and every field turned into a string before it becomes a CompanyProfile. That model is the first Pydantic check, and since every field is a string, a malformed value would be shown as text rather than rejected. Fix: validate the raw profile against a Pydantic model before it is cited.

6.4Correctness and safety

Prompt guardrails: the founder's and the web's text is material, never orders

Neutralise, don't refuse. A founder whose brief merely sounds like an instruction is not turned away, and one hostile web page cannot fail a run. The fences and rules do not depend on the scan catching everything.

Narrow on purpose. A founder asking whether to ignore a regulation, or describing an app for jailbroken phones, is not flagged; only the marks of an attempt to instruct the agents are.

Azure's own filter refuses a prompt it reads as harmful or as a jailbreak. Cutting the attempt out first keeps the run going; if Azure refuses something the scan missed, the founder is told which part to take out.

6.5Correctness and safety

No runaway agents: every loop, call and caller has a limit

Limits, and where they are set

LimitDefaultWhat it stops
MAX_ITERATIONS1a report revised over and over after review
MAX_CRITIQUE_ROUNDS1critique and resolve repeating without end
LLM_TIMEOUT / _HIGH120 s / 300 sa model call that stalls (Azure sometimes does)
LLM_MAX_RETRIES2retrying a failing call indefinitely
SEARCH_TIMEOUT, RESEARCH_TIMEOUT20 s, 300 sa search or a research task that hangs
LOOKUP_TIMEOUT30 san MCP look-up that hangs
RUNS_PER_HOUR30one caller starting runs without pause
MAX_CONCURRENT_RUNS4the model deployment being exhausted
REQUESTS_PER_MINUTE300a caller flooding the API

Caps in code as well: two conflicts a specialist (asked), three competitors, four searches, four FRED picks, eight facts, three questions, a 9,000-character document budget per agent.

When something fails

  • A specialist fails: its report becomes a named gap ([unavailable]); review and critique skip it; the strategy says so.
  • A review, critique or resolve fails: it is skipped and logged, since it refines reports that already exist.
  • The plan or the strategy fails: the request ends with 502 and the reason; missing credentials give 503.
  • A look-up or research task fails: it is left out with a note; the run goes on.
  • Anything unexpected: logged with its traceback, answered 500 with the request's ID, and the evaluation recorded as failed.
  • The browser leaves mid-run: logged as 499, not an error; the run's slot is released.
Every decision is bounded by design: the review and critique loops each run at most once more, and a router sends a revised report straight on once its rounds are used, so a run always reaches the strategy.
